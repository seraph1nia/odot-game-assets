# D01 — Silhouette, proportions and role hierarchy

## Evidence and observation

[Reference IDs](../references/index.md): V1–V7, A1/A3/A4, B1/B2/B5,
F4/F8 identify a dominant mass or gesture before small dressing: tree canopy,
bread cottage, mine opening, helmet/shield, hat/staff, lookout, cannon,
dome/telescope, civic bell, large mushroom cap or floating crystal.
V5/V6/V7 have canonical board crops; [C-ROOF](../references/evidence.md#crops)
shows roof/sign/lookout hierarchy. Pictures show toy-like, compact units with
readable faces/hands and substantial equipment, not anatomical realism.

## Standard and rationale

Define primary silhouette and primary role cue before detail. Preserve their
separation at the review sizes owned by [D08](presentation.md#d08).
Use chunky miniature proportions and deliberate height tiers; supporting detail
must not compete with the role cue. Do not normalize buildings to realistic
soldier-to-door ratios: these are tabletop miniatures.
Role discrimination matters more than close-up object count.

## Scope and exceptions

All 3D families inherit this rule. Unit landmarks exclude base and headgear when
assessing the body; props prioritize functional silhouette/grip; scenery needs a
focal mass and quiet space. V2's red roof and V1's tree-led silhouette remain
role-specific exceptions, not defects to turn into blue-roof cottages.

## Qualitative examples

- **Pass anchor:** B2's dome+telescope differs from A5's crystal spire;
  V5's shield/sword differs from V7's bow; A4's cannon muzzle is the upper focal mass.
- **Fail criterion:** a telescope shrunk behind dormers or crossbow merged into
  torso silhouette, even if screws/arrows are intricately modeled. A large role
  label does not repair the mesh's hierarchy.
- Review a silhouette/grayscale companion as well as the lit image. Explain any
  deliberate hidden cue or pose-dependent role reading instead of silently passing.

## Uncertainty and calibration

No exact head/body, weapon/hand or roof/wall ratio is extracted from one view.
Perspective, plume, hat and equipped pose change visible bounds. Mark comparable
body/structure landmarks, normalize occupied height, and compare several family
references before proposing a range. Pose-controlled blockouts and actual target
views require authorized evidence work; a still cannot establish temporal gestures.
